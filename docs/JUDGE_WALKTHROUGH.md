# ORBIT-M — Judge Walkthrough (November 14–15, 2026)

## The single-sentence story
**ORBIT-M notices a repeated change relative to the astronaut's own preflight baseline, explains what triggered it, and makes the next *authorized* check easier to discuss.**

This is a **software demonstration, not a validated medical device**. No NASA readings, medical diagnoses, medical clearance, private communications or actual referrals are performed.

## One-minute live demonstration (no manual form entry needed)

Open https://bzeira.github.io/orbit-m-space-apps-2026/#flightStory in a fresh/incognito window and click **Start judge demo**.

1. **Day 12 — routine:** fictional pulse and sleep are close to the synthetic preflight baseline. No team-made alert activates. Explain: “we do not treat every change as danger.”
2. **Day 15 — early shift:** sleep drops from the personal ~7.5 h baseline to ~6.1 h and pulse is higher. Neither repeated-change rule is met yet. Explain: “we monitor a trend, rather than label one reading as a diagnosis.”
3. **Day 17 — review signals:** two consecutive fictional readings exceed the **project-made** 20% pulse-change rule and show sleep more than 1.5 hours below baseline. The existing engine emits two explainable rule results. Say explicitly: “These thresholds are illustrative, not NASA-certified or clinically validated.”
4. **Day 19 — follow-up:** change persists. Show the *uncertainty* text and the suggested context questions. Click **Record fictional follow-up reviewed** only to indicate this view has been reviewed locally; it sends no message.
5. **End demo & restore my dashboard:** restores the user's previous dashboard readings instead of overwriting local history.

Optionally click **Inspect the linked vital signs dashboard** to show that the story actually reuses the production prototype engine and metrics, not just a prerecorded animation.

## Exact values and provenance
- Preflight baseline: 14 **synthetic** records generated by the team's existing \`makeScenario("stable")\` function.
- Inflight story: **project-authored fictional** readings for mission days 10–19 in \`src/mission-demo.mjs\`.
- Flags: computed by the existing \`analyze()\` function using two successive readings, not hard-coded to show.
- Heart rate flag: both latest fictional readings exceed **120% of the person's preflight pulse median**. **Unvalidated demo rule.**
- Sleep flag: both latest fictional readings are more than **1.5 hours below the preflight sleep median**. **Unvalidated demo rule.**
- This walkthrough intentionally does **not** demonstrate physiological diagnosis, DCS screening, treatment, automated intervention, or real cognitive assessment.

NASA background supports studying crew health over mission phases, including individual preflight measurements; NASA does **not** validate the project's artificial thresholds or sample values:
- https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/smd/space-life-physical-sciences-research-applications-division/human-research-program/spaceflight-standard-measures-characterizing-how-humans-adapt-in-space/
- NASA Human Research Program: https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/risks/

## What the judges can verify from public code
- \`src/mission-demo.mjs\`: pure deterministic scene generator uses the **same** health-monitoring \`analyze()\` function as the dashboard.
- \`src/mission-demo-ui.mjs\`: interactive stage buttons and chart; no external libraries, no remote API calls.
- \`src/app.mjs\`: temporarily projects scenes into the dashboard and restores prior local data at the end, without saving storyline values to localStorage.
- \`tests/mission-demo.test.mjs\`: baseline invariance, early change vs repeated-change alert, script determinism, scene bounds and no diagnosis claims.
- \`tests/interface-contract.test.mjs\`: presence of the interface and integration hooks.
- \`.github/workflows/ci.yml\`: syntax checks + Node built-in unit tests.

## Six-minute local judging plan
**4-minute pitch:**
- **0:00–0:35** The problem in a long-duration mission: difficult to recognize relevant personal changes without creating alarms for every reading.
- **0:35–0:55** The proposed solution: personal synthetic preflight median, interpretable longitudinal monitoring, local-first design.
- **0:55–2:05** Live judge walkthrough: day 12 → day 15 → day 17 → day 19. Show the flags, why they activated and what the app cannot infer.
- **2:05–2:45** Wider prototype: short overview of post-EVA symptoms, behavioral check-ins and cardiovascular tracking; do not open all forms.
- **2:45–3:20** Validity: exactly what NASA research informs vs what the team invented; running tests; data privacy caveat.
- **3:20–4:00** Why it matters, what is implemented, what needs real sensors and human-subjects validation.

**2-minute Q&A:**
- “Where are the NASA data?” NASA documentation informs what to monitor, while all current patient records and threshold rules are **fictional**. No real NASA biomedical dataset is integrated.
- “Is this a medical AI?” No. Transparent rule-based prototype. No learned model, diagnosis, validated risk score or replacement for a flight surgeon.
- “Why your thresholds?” Illustrative design values explicitly identified as unvalidated. The purpose is to demonstrate the explainable interface and data flow.
- “What about privacy?” The demo uses synthetic browser-local records. localStorage is **not suitable for actual confidential medical data**.
- “What works offline?” A scoped service worker now pre-caches static HTML/CSS/JS after the first successful online visit. All synthetic processing stays local. The team must **manually demonstrate an actual offline reload** before claiming offline availability. External NASA links still require a network.
