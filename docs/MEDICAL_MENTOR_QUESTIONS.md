# Questions for a clinician or mission health mentor (Nov 15)

This is a **prototype review**, not a request for medical approval. Ask the expert to help us accurately describe monitoring limits.

1. Of sleep duration, resting pulse, blood pressure, mood, and temperature, which **two** yield the most defensible short self-monitoring demo for a long mission? Why?
2. Which changes reflect normal microgravity adaptation rather than illness? What additional measurement context (posture, schedule, sensor, mission day) is essential?
3. Would a 14-day preflight median be a useful **illustration** of a personal baseline? What are its limitations, and how does a real protocol differ?
4. Which warning conditions may **never** rely on baseline comparisons? (Cabin O2, pressure, EVA decompression, radiation, severe symptoms.)
5. Which follow-up questions are useful without implying a diagnosis or dispensing treatment?
6. How should a private mental-health check-in be offered? Who can access it? What can be safely kept offline, and what requires professional support?
7. Which NASA research publication or available dataset can we meaningfully use this weekend, instead of merely quoting it?
8. What is one risky or scientifically unsupported claim in the current dashboard that we should remove?

**Write down the expert's exact role and advice with consent.** Don't advertise clinical approval, NASA endorsement or validated thresholds unless formally obtained.
